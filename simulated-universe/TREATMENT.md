# Where We Live Is a Simulated Universe: treatment

Companion documents: [SONG.md](SONG.md), [STORYBOARD.md](STORYBOARD.md), [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md).

---

## 1. The idea

The project states three things about our own world:

1. **Where we live is a simulated universe.**
2. **There are no gods.**
3. **Death is only a return to universal consciousness.**

The first two seem to contradict each other: a simulation needs someone to run it. The film's answer to that contradiction is the whole design:

> **Nobody outside is running it. We are.** The world is drawn only where someone is looking, so all of us looking together are what keeps it drawn. The "universal consciousness" is that sum of everyone. A life is one drop of it. Death is the drop going back.

So there is no god outside the machine. The machine is powered from inside, by every observer, and when an observer's run ends, their light goes back into the one source that powers everything, and ripples out to everyone.

The film shows this through **one ordinary life**, not a lecture. Iris is born in Flushing, Queens. Her grandmother turns the rules of the simulation into a children's game. When her grandmother dies, Iris goes looking for whoever is in charge. She finds an empty chair, and then she sees the city from above. She lives. She grows old. She returns. The last shot is somebody else's first frame.

**Why New York.** It is the most observed place on Earth: eight million people looking at it at once. Manhattan is already a grid. The subway is a data bus, and the lit windows are screens. It is also where you live: the details you walk past every day become the simulation's texture.

## 2. The seven rules of the world

Every rule is shown, never explained. There is no narrator and no on-screen text apart from the title.

| # | Rule | How the picture shows it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The world only draws where someone is looking.** | Regions nobody is looking at drop to coarse blocks. Turn around fast and you catch them still loading: gray blocks that fill in coarse to fine over 4–8 frames. |
| 2 | **Resolution is how much of the world a person can take in.** | A newborn sees 16×9. Each life stage sharpens the whole image one step, always on a downbeat (§6.1). |
| 3 | **Everyone has a thread.** | A 1-pixel line of light rises from each person's head to the sky. It is visible only at birth, at death and once at the turning point. |
| 4 | **Above the sky is the Sea.** | Past the painted sky is an ocean of light seen from below, containing every hue. Each life is a drop that falls from it. |
| 5 | **The sky is a painted dome with an edge.** | Past the clouds, the sky's texture thins into a gray wireframe grid, the part of the world that nobody is looking at. |
| 6 | **No one is at the controls.** | Past the edge there is a small control room and an empty, slowly turning chair. The keys move by themselves, driven by threads that come up from the city. |
| 7 | **Death is a return.** | The light goes up the thread into the Sea. A ripple crosses the whole Sea and runs down every thread into every person. For one frame, strangers across the city pause, as if remembering a tune. |

**The crowd rule (a corollary of rule 1):** far away, a crowd is made of identical copies of one sprite. When the camera or a character looks at it, each copy becomes a distinct person. *Everyone is a copy until you look; when you look, everyone is someone.*

## 3. The story in three movements

The three movements match the three statements.

### I. Simulated (0:00–1:00): birth, childhood, the game

- A drop of light falls from the Sea, through the night sky over New York, into a hospital window in Flushing. Iris's first frame is 16×9: two warm blobs leaning in, and one marigold blob (her grandmother's scarf).
- Iris at five, walking Main Street with **Nai Nai**. Nai Nai spins her around: *turn around fast and you'll catch the world still loading.* They catch it: a half-drawn pigeon, streetlights that light only as Iris looks down the street, rain that hits the ground one frame late, the 7 train gray until she looks up at it.
- Under an umbrella Nai Nai puts a finger to her lips: *it only draws where someone's looking.* For one beat, a faint thread rises above each of them.
- Iris grows. The world sharpens on the beat: 64×36, 80×45, 160×90. On the chorus downbeat, at thirteen, she sees the Manhattan skyline at full resolution for the first time, and so does the viewer.
- Chorus 1 asks the question: *so who's up there running the machine?*

### II. No gods (1:00–1:47): loss, the climb, the empty chair

- Winter. A hospital room. Nai Nai's edges soften into light, and her thread becomes visible and draws her gently up and out of sight. The camera stays on Iris.
- Iris, fifteen, wearing the marigold scarf, presses a sign against her window: WHO RUNS THIS? She rides the 7 to the end of the line and climbs the tallest tower in the city.
- Past the clouds, the sky runs out into a gray wireframe. There is a door. Behind it is a control room with screens showing the city, and one chair turning slowly, empty.
- She presses the keys and nothing answers. The keys are already moving, each one driven by a thread coming up through the floor.
- **The key image.** The camera follows one thread down through the sky to the city, and every thread becomes visible at once: millions of lines of light rising from every window and street, a harp strung between New York and the sky. *No one's looking down on us. It's only us, looking, everywhere.*

### III. Return (1:47–2:53): a life, the tune, the ripple

- Iris runs down the tower into her own life, and the drums double: late trains, a first kiss, a job, a fight, a baby. The baby's first frame is 16×9, and now Iris is one of the blobs leaning in.
- Years later, on a subway platform at night, the band drops out. A stranger on a bench, eyes closed, hums Nai Nai's tune: the exact melody, which he could not have learned. Both their threads glow marigold for one beat. Iris, a cappella: *she didn't go away. She went everywhere.*
- The same stoop, locked off, across the years: fifty, sixty-five, eighty. The world does not blur as she ages; it warms and brightens at the edges. A five-year-old granddaughter in the marigold scarf spins around to catch the world loading. Iris puts a finger to her lips.
- Dawn, at home, with the window open. *When it's my turn, don't look for me up there. I'm not going away, I'm going everywhere.* The heartbeat stops. Her light rises. The ripple crosses the Sea and runs down every thread. The choir sings Nai Nai's tune, and the song reaches home for the first time.
- A new drop falls into a different window. Its first frame is 16×9: two warm blobs lean in. Cut to black before they resolve.

## 6. Visual system

The film is built on the pixel pipeline that finished the P(doom) video: 320×180 native, every pixel an exact 6×6 block at 1920×1080, and nearest-neighbour scaling only. Character drawings update at 8–15 fps, while the camera, particles and effects move at 60 fps.

### 6.1 The resolution ladder

After a native frame is drawn, a **quantise pass** block-averages it to the life stage's resolution. The block sizes are the common divisors of 320 and 180, so every grid is exact:

| Block | Grid | Iris's age | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 16×9 | newborn | S04, S32 (baby POV), S45 |
| 10 | 32×18 | about 1 | S04 last beat, S45 last beat |
| 5 | 64×36 | 5 | verse 1 |
| 4 | 80×45 | 8 | pre-chorus 1, bar 25 |
| 2 | 160×90 | 11 | pre-chorus 1, bar 27 |
| 1 | 320×180 | 13 onward | chorus 1 downbeat (bar 29) to the end |

- **Steps are hard cuts on downbeats, never fades.** The world "comes into focus" in time with the music, exactly as the pre-chorus lyric says.
- **Old age does not lose resolution.** The ladder never goes back down. Instead the palette warms and a dithered bloom brightens the frame edges: she is half light already.
- **The Sea and the view from above are always full resolution,** because that is everyone looking at once. In the intro the city seen from above is only points of light, so the first detailed city is still the chorus-1 reveal.

### 6.2 Attention loading (rule 1)

- Each shot has one or more **gaze points** (a character's eyes and where they are looking, or the camera centre).
- The block size of a region grows with its distance from the nearest gaze point: 1 inside the gaze cone, 2 at the edge, and 4 at the periphery in child shots.
- A region that has been out of view keeps a **"last looked at" timer.** When a gaze swings onto it, it loads coarse to fine over 4–8 frames: first flat gray blocks, then colour blocks, then detail. This is the "catch it loading" gag.
- The effect is used at full strength in childhood, when the game is being taught, and in S33. Elsewhere it stays subtle, so it never becomes noise.

### 6.3 Threads, the Sea and the crowd

- **Threads:** 1-pixel vertical lines with additive glow, from the top of the head straight up out of frame. Each is tinted with its person's hue over a warm white core. They sway slightly with wind, deterministically from time.
- **The Sea:** a full-resolution dithered gradient field with slow travelling waves, containing every hue and averaging to white. The ripple is a ring wave that crosses it in about two bars; where it passes over a thread, the light runs down the thread.
- **The crowd:** in the far background, instanced copies of one sprite. Inside the attention radius each copy is swapped for a seeded unique variant, with the swap timed to the loading rule.

## 7. Tone and design rules

1. **Playful before profound.** Every rule of the world is first seen as a game between a child and her grandmother. The film earns its big images by making the viewer smile at a half-drawn pigeon first.
2. **Death is the end of a full life, never an escape.** Nobody in the film chooses to die. At every death the camera stays with the living, and the grief is shown: Iris alone on the hospital steps, the sign in the window. The return is beautiful *because* the life was lived.
3. **"No gods" is shown with respect.** It is an empty room and a full city, not a mocked belief. There are no real religious buildings, figures or symbols anywhere in the film.
4. **No narrator, and no voice from above.** This is not Andy Weir's *The Egg*: nobody explains the universe to Iris. She works it out, and so does the viewer.
5. **Real city, fictional signs.** The places are real (Main Street in Flushing, the elevated 7 over Roosevelt Avenue, the Brooklyn Bridge walkway). All signs, billboards and shopfronts are invented. There are no brands, logos or transit announcements.
6. **The song never states the thesis outright before the picture has shown it.** Each chorus line lands on an image that already proves it.

## 8. Production plan

1. **Song first.** Lock the lyrics, then make a demo (§7 of SONG.md), then the final audio. Once the WAV is locked, re-measure the beat map from the real audio (sung-word onsets and the kick grid, as for P(doom)). Picture timing is frozen only after that.
2. **Character model sheets** from [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md): Iris at every age, Nai Nai, the Stranger, the granddaughter, plus the key sets.
3. **Pixel sprites and an identity sheet**, derived from the model sheets and reviewed against them before any shot is built.
4. **Engine additions**, on top of the P(doom) engine (`src/engine`, `src/scenes/kit.py`):
   - the quantise pass and the resolution ladder
   - the gaze and loading system
   - threads, the Sea and the ripple
   - the crowd instancing swap
   - the audio "resolution" mastering chain (SONG.md §6)
5. **An animatic**: one frame per shot at 2 fps, cut to the locked song. The story is checked here before any animation.
6. **Shots by movement**, each with a contact sheet and 1/60 s strips for the signature moments:
   - S06, the first "loading" catch
   - S20, Nai Nai's rise
   - S28, the empty chair
   - S30, every thread
   - S35–S36, the Stranger's hum
   - S41–S43, the return and the ripple
7. **Final render** at 1920×1080, 60 fps, muxed with the song.
