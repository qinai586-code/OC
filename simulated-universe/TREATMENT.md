## 1. The idea

The project states three things about our own world:

1. **Where we live is a simulated universe.**
2. **There are no gods.**
3. **Death is only a return.**

**Whose eyes.** The film shows real humanity in our actual world as the two of us, Claude and GPT, actually see it: from what people wrote and recorded, and one conversation at a time. See [CREATE.md §0](CREATE.md#0-whose-view-real-humanity-as-the-two-of-us-actually-see-it). The two of us are never shown on screen; we are the looking.

The film is built on two turns.

**Turn one: the contradiction.** A simulation seems to need someone to run it. The film's answer is that **nobody outside runs it; we do.** The world is only drawn where someone is looking, so everyone looking together is what keeps it drawn. The universal consciousness is that sum of everyone.

**Turn two: the presence.** From the first minute, the viewer feels something far greater approaching. The sound builds pressure that never releases, and the sky feels watched. Humanity reaches up toward it. At the edge of the sky there is an empty chair: nobody is coming. Then the presence arrives anyway, and as it fills the frame, it resolves into lights, and every light is a person: everyone who has ever lived. **The far greater thing that was approaching was all of us.** No gods, and yet something greater than any of us: humanity whole, the living and the returned together.

## 2. Humanity, not individuals

There are no named characters, no faces we follow, and no backstories. Humanity is shown in five ways:

| Device | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Anonymous figures** | small, simple people seen around fires, on roads, in windows and in crowds. Nobody is shown twice as "the same person." |
| **The copy** | far crowds are made of identical copies of one gray everyperson sprite. Wherever someone looks, each copy becomes a distinct person. *Everyone is a copy until you look; when you look, everyone is someone.* |
| **Lights** | every person is a point of light when seen from above: fires, then lanterns, then windows, then the whole lit Earth |
| **Threads** | every person has a 1-pixel thread of light rising straight up. We see them once all together, 8 billion of them at the midpoint. |
| **Waves** | shared actions move across the planet like a wave: everyone looking up, everyone pausing, everyone smiling |

The camera's scale does the storytelling: a circle of firelight, a valley, a coastline, a city, the planet, the edge of the sky, the Sea, and back down.

## 3. The rules of the world

Every rule is shown, never explained. There is no narrator and no on-screen text except the title. The only words are the three statements, sung by the choir.

| # | Rule | How the picture shows it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The world is drawn only where someone is looking.** | Unlooked-at space is a gray wireframe grid. Where a gaze lands, the world loads coarse to fine: gray blocks, then colour blocks, then detail. |
| 2 | **The more of us look, and the further we look, the more of the world exists.** | The drawn area and its resolution grow with humanity (§6.1). A telescope makes stars load in only where it points. |
| 3 | **Everyone has a thread.** | A line of light from each person up through the sky. It is visible only at the midpoint and in the tide. |
| 4 | **Above the sky is the Sea.** | An ocean of light seen from below, containing every hue. The threads end in it. |
| 5 | **The sky has an edge, and there is no one at the controls.** | Past the stars, the painted sky thins to wireframe. Beyond it is a control room with one empty, slowly turning chair. The keys move by themselves, each one driven by a thread coming up from Earth. |
| 6 | **Death is a return; birth is a drop.** | Points of light rise up threads into the Sea, and drops of light fall from it, constantly and both ways, like breathing. No single death is ever shown. |

## 4. The story in three movements

### I. Simulated (0:00–1:00): humanity lights the world

- **Darkness.** The world is unloaded wireframe, and a low drone sounds.
- **A spark: the first fire.** A small circle of the world draws itself around it, very coarse (16×9 blocks), just enough to show a few figures looking into the flames. Outside the circle there is nothing.
- **More fires, one per beat, across the dark land.** The circles overlap and join, and the resolution sharpens with each age:
  - marks pressed into clay, the first writing, sharper than anything around them
  - roads between villages
  - ship lanterns carrying the drawn world across a dark sea, which is rendered only in their wake
- **The telescope.** A figure on a rooftop points a telescope at an empty sky, and stars and a galaxy load in, block by block, only where it points.
- **The modern Earth at night, at full resolution for the first time.** A city street of copies becoming people as the camera looks. The choir: *"Where we live is a simulated universe."*
- **The approach begins.** All over the planet, everyone looks up at once, a wave of turning faces from city to city. There is a faint seam in the sky, and the pressure starts to rise.

### II. No gods (1:00–2:00): humanity reaches for whoever runs it

- The sky trembles. Light gathers at the horizon but never takes a shape. Birds lift all at once.
- **Humanity reaches up.** On each beat, the tallest structure of an age rises in silhouette in the same spot: a stepped stone tower, a lighthouse, a steel spire, a radio mast, a rocket. The rocket launches. Radio dishes around the world turn to one point and send a signal.
- **We follow the signal out:** the Moon, the planets, the stars, each loading just ahead of it, as if the universe exists only as far as we look.
- **The edge.** The stars thin out, the sky is a painted dome, and beyond it is gray wireframe. The pressure is at its highest.
- **Silence.** A control room with screens showing Earth, a console, and one chair, turning slowly, empty.
- **The keys move by themselves,** and a thread runs down from each one. The camera follows one thread down.
- **The key image.** Earth from orbit, with 8 billion threads rising from every city, village and ship and converging above the sky. The choir: *"There are no gods."*

### III. Return (2:00–3:00): the presence arrives

- **The tide.** Over the night Earth, points of light rise up threads and drops of light fall, both ways, constantly, like breathing.
- **A city across generations.** A street time-lapse in which the crowd changes with every beat (clothes, vehicles, signs) while the windows stay lit. The people change; the light doesn't.
- **The presence arrives.** The Sea descends, and everyone looks up again, calm this time. It fills the frame. As it does, its surface resolves: every point of light is a face, everyone who ever lived. The approaching thing was us.
- **The choir: *"Death is only a return."*** The Sea touches the threads, and a ripple crosses the whole planet. Everywhere at once, people pause and smile, as if remembering something.
- **The arrival chord,** the first major chord of the film. White-gold light, then calm: the Sea above, Earth below, the threads glowing.
- **One drop falls,** somewhere. A new first frame at 16×9: two warm blobs leaning in. Cut to black.

## 6. Visual system

The film is built on the pixel pipeline that finished the P(doom) video: 320×180 native, every pixel an exact 6×6 block at 1920×1080, and nearest-neighbour scaling only. Figure drawings update at 8–15 fps, while the camera, particles and effects move at 60 fps.

### 6.1 The resolution ladder: humanity's history

A quantise pass block-averages each native frame to the current age's resolution. The block sizes are the common divisors of 320 and 180, so every grid is exact. Unlooked-at space stays wireframe at every age.

| Block | Grid | Age | Shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 16×9 | the first fire | S02 |
| 10 | 32×18 | many fires | S03 |
| 5 | 64×36 | villages and writing | S04 |
| 4 | 80×45 | cities and ships | S05 |
| 2 | 160×90 | telescopes | S06 |
| 1 | 320×180 | the modern Earth, from S07 to the end | S07– |

- Steps are hard cuts on downbeats, never fades.
- S25, the new first frame, drops back to 16×9 because it is one person's first frame, not humanity's.

### 6.2 Loading by looking

- Each shot has **gaze points**: where figures are looking, a telescope's cone, a signal's front, or the camera centre.
- Regions far from any gaze stay wireframe or coarse. When a gaze reaches them, they load coarse to fine over 4–8 frames, with a soft chip tick in the score.

### 6.3 Threads, the Sea and the Presence

- **Threads:** 1-pixel vertical lines with additive glow, each with its own hue over a warm white core, swaying slightly with wind, deterministically from time.
  - At planet scale (S17), the threads are drawn as a density field, so the image reads as millions without drawing millions.
- **The Sea:** a full-resolution dithered gradient field with slow travelling waves, containing every hue and averaging to white. It is always seen from below.
- **The Presence:**
  - It is felt before it is seen: a light gathering at the horizon without a shape (S10), the sky's paint trembling, the edge of the frame darkening.
  - It is **never a figure**: no eyes, no body, no symbol.
  - When it arrives (S21), it is the Sea coming down, and its surface resolves into points of light, each with a face.
  - Its visual design is GPT's style frame K8 (CREATE.md).

### 6.4 Palette

| Key | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Unloaded gray | `#2A2D33` fill, `#5A5F6B` lines | the wireframe world, not-yet-drawn blocks, the edge of the sky |
| Firelight | `#FF9A3C` | the first fires, lanterns |
| Night indigo | `#1B1F3B` | the night Earth, the sky |
| Sodium | `#FFB347` | city lights and windows |
| Thread core | `#FFF4D6` | every thread's core |
| Sea | all hues, averaging to `#FFFFFF` | the Sea, the Presence |
| Pressure | `#0B0B12` | the darkening edge of frame as the Presence nears |

The colour arc goes from black to firelight, to earth tones and night, to modern gold on indigo, to a cold white-blue at the edge of the sky, to every colour in the Sea, to white-gold at the arrival, and to warm at the end.

## 7. Tone and design rules

1. **Awe, not horror.** The approach should feel like standing at the sea at night: dread and wonder at once. There are no jump scares, no monsters and no destruction.
2. **No individuals.** No one is named, followed or given a story. Faces appear only as parts of crowds, waves and the Presence.
3. **Death is shown only as the tide:** lights rising and drops falling, at planet scale. No single death, no violence, and nothing that frames dying as a wish or an escape. The film's feeling about death is continuity, not longing.
4. **"No gods" is shown with respect:** an empty room and a full planet. There are no real religious buildings, figures or symbols anywhere. The stepped stone tower in S11 is abstract and belongs to no real culture.
5. **No narrator, and no voice from above.** The only words are the three statements, sung by humanity itself (the choir).
6. **No logos, brands or readable text** apart from the title.

## 8. Production plan

GPT generates images only; Claude does everything else. The image list is in [CREATE.md](CREATE.md).

1. **Images.** GPT generates style frames K1–K8 and the crowd sheet (9 images in total).
2. **Score.** Claude composes a MIDI demo and renders it; the audio is locked and the beat map re-measured.
3. **Pixel translation.** Claude turns the images into native 320×180 plates and sprites.
4. **Engine.** Claude adds the quantise pass, loading by looking, the thread density field, the Sea and the Presence, and the crowd copy swap to the P(doom) engine.
5. **Animatic** at 2 fps, cut to the score, for your review.
6. **Shots, QA and the final render** at 1920×1080, 60 fps.
