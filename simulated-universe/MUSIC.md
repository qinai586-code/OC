# Where We Live Is a Simulated Universe: score

> **Superseded in part:** the art style is no longer pixel art, and the song is now made in Suno. See [CREATE.md](CREATE.md). The story and shot order still hold; timings will be re-cut to the chosen Suno take.

An original score, about 3:00, mostly instrumental with a choir. The only words are the three statements, sung by the choir, one per movement.

## 1. The feeling

**The target:** the sense of an unknown presence, as if something far greater is approaching. This is the same feeling as the background music of the earlier pixel video (P(doom)).

That video's audio was never committed to the repo, so this score is designed from the feeling you described, not from the track. It shares no melody, lyric or structure with it. If you can point me to the exact moment of that video you mean, I'll match its texture, tempo and instrumentation more closely.

**Awe, not horror.** It should feel like standing at the sea at night: dread and wonder at once. The pressure builds for two minutes. Then it is cut to silence, and then it finally arrives and turns out to be made of human voices.

## 2. How "something far greater is approaching" is built

| Technique | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **A sub-bass drone on D** under the whole film | the floor of reality: always there, felt more than heard | bars 1–54 |
| **The rising tone that never arrives:** a Shepard–Risset glissando, a stack of octave layers each fading in at the bottom and out at the top | the ear hears an endless ascent: something always getting closer that never gets here. This is the core device. | builds bars 17–28; returns at 41 and, for the first time, **lands** |
| **Approach by distance:** low brass (tuba, bass trombone, contrabass horn) swelling pp→ff→pp every two bars | each swell starts with less reverb, a brighter tone and a slightly louder level, so it sounds physically nearer each time | bars 19–28 |
| **The flat second:** E♭ grinding against the D drone (Phrygian) | the oldest sound of unease; the harmony leans on something that isn't home | bars 17–28 |
| **Many hearts out of phase:** 8–16 recorded heartbeats and footfalls, each looping at a slightly different rate (69–75 per minute) | humanity as a pulse: busy, uncoordinated, alive | from bar 5; they drift into perfect sync by bar 43 |
| **The machinery:** soft square-wave chip ticks | the simulation's clock, heard only when the world loads (the pixel video's family sound) | the loading moments; alone in the silence at bars 31–32 |
| **Silence** | after two minutes of pressure, everything stops except the drone. The empty room is the loudest moment of the film. | bars 29–32 |
| **The reveal in sound** | when the Presence arrives, the synthesised rising tone crossfades into a real choir singing the same pitches. The approaching sound was human voices all along. | bars 41–46 |

## 4. Harmony

**The rule:** the drone is D for the whole film. The major third, F♯, is withheld until **bar 47**. Until then the harmony moves from open fifths (primal), to minor (the human world), to the flat second (dread), to the raised fourth (wonder). When F♯ finally sounds, it is the arrival.

| Bars | Harmony over the D drone |
|---|---|
| 1–12 | open fifths only (D–A), no third: primal and undecided |
| 13–16 | Dm(add9) · B♭maj7/D · C/D · Dm(add9): the human world, beautiful and minor |
| 17–28 | Dm · E♭maj7/D · Gm/D · E♭/D, repeated, thickening: **Phrygian dread**, the E♭ grinding on the D |
| 29–32 | the drone and a high A harmonic only |
| 33–36 | B♭maj7/D · C/D · Dm(add9) · held: the E♭ is gone and the dread eases |
| 37–40 | Dm · C · B♭ · C (the tide ostinato) |
| 41–44 | D5 with G♯ (♯11) above: **Lydian wonder**, still no third |
| 45–46 | E/D, the Lydian II over the drone, under statement 3 |
| **47–50** | **D major (add ♯11): D F♯ A G♯. The first F♯ of the film.** |
| 51–54 | D(add9) decaying to the drone alone |

## 5. The three sung lines

Sung by the choir (humanity's voice), in unison, low (altos, tenors and basses), slowly, two bars each.

| Bars | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 15–16 | **Where we live is a simulated universe.** | the street of copies becoming people |
| 33–34 | **There are no gods.** | 8 billion threads |
| 45–46 | **Death is only a return.** | the Presence resolves into everyone |

**Many languages becoming one.** In the bar *before* each line (14, 32 and 44), the same line is whispered by many voices in many languages at once, a murmur like a crowd. It converges into the unison English line on the downbeat.

| Language | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | Where we live is a simulated universe. | There are no gods. | Death is only a return. |
| 中文 | 我们所在的，是一个模拟宇宙。 | 没有神。 | 死亡只是回归。 |
| 日本語 | 私たちが生きているのは、シミュレーションの宇宙。 | 神はいない。 | 死は、ただの帰還。 |
| Español | Donde vivimos es un universo simulado. | No hay dioses. | La muerte es solo un regreso. |

Add more languages recorded by native speakers. The more there are, the more the murmur sounds like everyone.

## 7. Brief for a music generator

**Style prompt** (paste as one line):

> dark ambient cinematic score, 72 BPM, D Phrygian, deep sub-bass drone, endlessly rising Shepard tone tension, distant low brass swells growing closer, many heartbeats pulsing out of sync, soft chiptune ticks, low strings, sparse celesta, massed unison choir singing three slow lines, sudden total silence at the peak, final overwhelming D major bloom, awe not horror, no drum kit, no EDM, no pop vocals

**Lyrics with structure tags:**

```
[Intro — ambient drone, no vocals]
[Build]
[Choir, unison, low]
Where we live is a simulated universe.
[Rising tension — no vocals]
[Silence]
[Choir, unison, low]
There are no gods.
[Piano, tide]
[Climax — choir]
Death is only a return.
[Bloom — wordless choir]
[Coda — near silence]
```

**What a generator will not do reliably:**
- the Shepard–Risset rise
- the heartbeat phasing
- the withheld F♯
- the multilingual murmur
- the exact bar counts

Claude builds those layers directly and adds them on top of any take in post. If a generated take is chosen, the beat map (§8) is re-measured from it before animation.

**The composed route:** Claude writes the whole score as MIDI (drone, rising tone, brass, strings, piano, celesta, chip, hearts, and the choir parts on a placeholder voice) and renders a demo. This locks form and timing. The real choir and the language murmur need recorded voices.
