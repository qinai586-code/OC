# "Where We Live Is a Simulated Universe": song

An original song written for the film: lyrics, form, harmony, melody sketch and production brief. It carries the film's story from first frame to return, and its musical rules act out the three statements (see §4 and §6).

## 1. Concept

- **One life, one song.** The song follows Iris from birth to return: childhood, loss, the climb, adulthood, old age and death, then a new life.
- **The heartbeat is the clock.** A heartbeat layer runs through the whole song and ages with her: fast in childhood, resting as an adult, slow in old age, then silent. A new, fast heartbeat begins in the last four bars (§6).
- **Home is withheld until the return.** The song is in E♭ major, but the home chord (E♭ in root position on a strong beat) never sounds until bar 97, when Iris returns to the Sea. Every chorus uses the IV–V–iii–vi "royal road" progression, which circles home without landing. The listener feels the pull for 2:40, and the return is the first time the song arrives. Then a new phrase starts, unresolved: a new life.
- **Nai Nai's tune is the proof of return.** A short lullaby motif is first heard on a music box, then hummed by a stranger who could not have learned it, then sung by a choir of everyone. Its last note lands on the home note only at the very end (§5.1).
- **The sound has resolution too.** The mix starts narrow, mono and low-bandwidth (a newborn's hearing) and opens up in steps, on the same downbeats as the picture (§6.2).

## 3. Full lyrics

Two bars per line unless marked. Iris sings every line; other voices are marked.

```
[INTRO — bars 1–8 — no lyrics]
        the Sea's hum; a fast heartbeat; music box plays Nai Nai's tune (bars 5–8)

[VERSE 1 — bars 9–24 — lighter, close, a memory]
 9–10   Turn around fast, Nai Nai said,
11–12   and you'll catch the world still loading —
13–14   a pigeon only half drawn in,
15–16   a streetlight not yet glowing.
17–18   The rain lands one frame late on Main Street,
19–20   the 7 train's still gray above;
21–22   she laughed and whispered, "Don't you tell:
23–24   it only draws where someone's looking, love."

[PRE-CHORUS 1 — bars 25–28]
25–26   So I kept my eyes wide open,
27–28   and it all came into focus —

[CHORUS 1 — bars 29–36]
29–30   Where we live is a simulated universe,
31–32   eight million windows, every one a screen,
33–34   every light's a someone keeping it lit —
35–36   so who's up there running the machine?

[VERSE 2 — bars 37–52]
37–38   Winter came, the hospital hummed,
39–40   her edges softened into light,
41–42   a thread I'd never seen before
43–44   drew her gently out of sight.
45–46   I asked the nurses, asked the night,
47–48   who's in charge here? Who do I call?
49–50   I rode the 7 to the end of the line
51–52   and climbed the tallest thing of all.

[PRE-CHORUS 2 — bars 53–56]
53–54   Past the clouds the sky runs out,
55–56   gray lines where the colours stop —

[CHORUS 2 — bars 57–64]
57–58   Where we live is a simulated universe:
59–60   I climbed up past the sky — an empty chair.
61–62   No one's looking down on us,
63–64   it's only us, looking, everywhere.

[BRIDGE — bars 65–76 — F major, double-time drums]
65–66   So I lived like the lights were mine to keep lit:
67–68   late trains, first kiss, a job, a fight, a kid —
69–70   I turned around fast just to catch it loading,
71–72   and laughed, 'cause now I'm in on it.
73–74   (the band drops out; a stranger hums Nai Nai's tune, first half)
75–76   (a cappella, to the second half of the tune)
        She didn't go away — she went everywhere.

[BREAKDOWN — bars 77–84 — E♭, felt piano]
77–78   My hands got soft, the colours warmed,
79–80   the edges of the world turned gold;
81–82   there's a girl now in a marigold scarf,
83–84   turning round fast, five years old.

[FINAL CHORUS — bars 85–96 — intimate over a growing choir]
85–86   Where we live is a simulated universe —
87–88   when it's my turn, don't look for me up there:
89–90   I'm not going away,
91–92   I'm going everywhere.
93–96   (choir of many voices, wordless: Nai Nai's tune, landing home on bar 97)

[OUTRO — bars 97–104 — no lyrics]
97–100  the home chord, for the first time; the choir holds
101–104 a new, fast heartbeat; the music box plays the tune's first bar once
```

**How the lyrics are built:**
- **Plant and payoff.** Nai Nai's *"it only draws where someone's looking"* (bar 23) becomes *"no one's looking down on us, it's only us, looking"* (bars 61–64).
- **The same payoff, one level deeper.** *"She didn't go away, she went everywhere"* (bar 75) becomes Iris's own *"I'm not going away, I'm going everywhere"* (bars 89–92).
- **Chorus 1 asks; chorus 2 answers.** *"Who's up there running the machine?"* is answered by *"an empty chair … it's only us."* The final chorus applies the answer to death.
- **Singable title.** The title line is the first line of every chorus: 12 syllables over 2 bars, with room to breathe.
- **Nothing is sung before it is seen.** Each line lands on the picture that proves it (see STORYBOARD.md).

## 4. Key plan and harmony

**The rule:** E♭ major. The E♭ chord may appear in inversion (E♭/G, E♭/B♭) but never in root position on a strong beat until **bar 97**. The bridge lifts to F major and also never lands on F.

| Section | Bars | Chords (one per bar unless split) |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–4 | A♭(add9) pad only, no bass |
| | 5–8 | A♭maj7 · B♭ · Cm7 · E♭/G (under the music-box tune) |
| Verse 1 | 9–16, 17–24 | A♭maj7 · E♭/G · Fm7 · B♭sus4 · A♭maj7 · E♭/G · Cm7 · B♭sus4→B♭ |
| Pre-chorus 1 | 25–28 | Cm7 · B♭/D · E♭/G→A♭ · B♭sus4→B♭ |
| Chorus 1 | 29–36 | **A♭maj7 · B♭ · Gm7 · Cm7** · Fm7 · Gm7 · A♭maj7 · B♭sus4→B♭ |
| Verse 2 | 37–44, 45–52 | Cm7 · A♭maj7 · Fm7 · Gsus4→G · Cm7 · A♭maj7 · Fm7 · B♭sus4→B♭ |
| Pre-chorus 2 | 53–56 | A♭ · B♭/A♭ · Gm7 · Csus4→**C** (a major chord outside the key, "past the sky") |
| Chorus 2 | 57–64 | as chorus 1; bars 63–64 hold B♭ under a choir "oo" (the first hint of the choir) |
| Bridge (F) | 65–72 | B♭maj7 · C · Am7 · Dm7 · Gm7 · Am7 · B♭maj7 · C |
| | 73–74 | no harmony: the hum alone, in E♭ |
| | 75–76 | a low A♭ pedal enters under the a cappella line, pulling back to E♭ |
| Breakdown | 77–84 | A♭maj7 · Gm7 · Fm7 · B♭sus4 · A♭maj7 · Gm7 · Fm7 · B♭sus4→B♭ |
| Final chorus | 85–92 | as chorus 1 |
| | 93–96 | A♭maj7 · B♭ · Gm7→Cm7 · A♭→B♭sus4 |
| Outro | 97–100 | **E♭ (root position), then E♭(add9)**: home |
| | 101–104 | E♭(add9) decaying; the music box alone on bar 104 |

- **The modulation to F is seamless.** Chorus 2 ends on B♭, which is V in E♭ and IV in F, so the bridge's first chord continues it without a seam. Adulthood feels like arriving somewhere new. It is interrupted before it lands.
- **The way back is Nai Nai's tune.** The Stranger hums it in E♭ over silence, and it pulls the song back down to its home key.
- **Verse 2's G major** (bar 40, *"her edges softened into light"*) is the one bright chord in the darkest verse, placed on the light.

## 5. Melody

### 5.1 Nai Nai's tune (the lullaby motif)

Four bars, in quarter notes unless marked, E♭ major:

```
bar 1          bar 2          bar 3          bar 4
G4 B♭4 C5 B♭4 | G4(h)  F4(h) | C5 B♭4 G4 F4 | G4 (whole)          ← every time until the end: ends on the 3rd, "not home yet"
G4 B♭4 C5 B♭4 | G4(h)  F4(h) | C5 B♭4 G4 F4 | F4 (whole, suspended) → E♭4 on bar 97   ← the choir, once
```

| Where | Bars | Instrument or voice |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 5–8 | music box |
| Verse 1 | 21–24 | music box, under *"she laughed and whispered"* |
| Bridge | 73–74 | the Stranger hums bars 1–2 |
| Bridge | 75–76 | Iris sings bars 3–4 to the words *"She didn't go away — she went everywhere"* |
| Final chorus | 93–97 | choir, with the resolving ending |
| Outro | 104 | music box, bar 1 only: a new life, unresolved |

### 5.2 The chorus hook (sketch)

This is the contour; the demo will settle the details.

```
            (bar 28, beat 4)   bar 29 (A♭maj7)                         bar 30 (B♭)
             Where  we        | live ——  is  a   sim  u   la  ted      | u   ni  verse ———
             B♭4    C5        | E♭5 ———  D5  C5  B♭4 C5  B♭4 G4       | G4  A♭4 B♭4 ———
```

- Line 2 (*"eight million windows…"*) repeats that shape a step lower.
- Line 3 climbs.
- Line 4 ends **on the leading tone D5** over B♭: *"running the ma-CHINE?"* The question is unresolved in the music as well as the words.
- Chorus 2 ends on B♭ (*"everywhere"*, open).
- The final chorus ends on F over B♭sus4 (*"I'm going everywhere"*, suspended), and the choir resolves it.

Iris's range is about B♭3 to F5, in a comfortable mezzo.

## 7. Instrumentation, vocals and the demo

**Genre:** a cinematic J-pop ballad that builds into an anthem, with a chamber-pop heart, felt piano, a heartbeat clock and New York itself as percussion. It should be bright, clean and emotional, never syrupy.

| Instrument | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Heartbeat layer** | the clock of one life (§6.1) |
| **Music box** | Nai Nai's tune in childhood |
| **Felt piano** | Iris's inner voice; carries verse 2 and the breakdown |
| **Soft chip arpeggios** (square and triangle, low in the mix) | the simulation's machinery. A quick run marks each "loading" catch; otherwise they are nearly hidden. |
| **Clean electric guitars, bass, drum kit** | the living city, from pre-chorus 1 on; double-time in the bridge; none in the breakdown |
| **Strings** (quartet, swelling to a section) | feeling: the hospital, the edge of the sky, every thread (bar 63) |
| **Choir** (many voices, mixed ages) | universal consciousness. A hint at bars 63–64; fully present only from bar 93. |
| **New York foley as percussion**, recorded fresh | a turnstile clack on off-beat accents, a crosswalk tick as the pre-chorus metronome, a door two-tone chime written into the key, pigeon wings on fills, rain, a radiator knock. No station announcements or other recordings. |

**Voices:**
- **Iris:** one female mezzo, natural and clear, with no robotic processing. Verse 1 is lighter and closer to the mic (a memory). The bridge is full chest voice. The breakdown is softer and older in texture. The final chorus is intimate, over the choir.
- **Nai Nai:** heard only in the intro as a faint hum under the music box; an older woman's voice.
- **The Stranger:** a warm baritone hum, eyes closed, unaccompanied (bars 73–74).

**The demo.** I can compose the full arrangement as MIDI (every part, with the tune and the chorus melody on a lead instrument) and render it to audio with a sampled instrument set. That locks the form, harmony, heartbeat and timing, and the storyboard can be cut to it. A sung vocal needs either a singer or a generator. The generator brief is below.
