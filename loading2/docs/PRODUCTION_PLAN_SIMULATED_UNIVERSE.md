### Discrepancies to rule on (they are not mine to settle)

- **D1. The main Final Chorus is not sung in this track.** Between "not I" (2:43.1) and "And one day"
  (2:50.3) there is a 1 s gap, a held high vowel and choir. No window from 2:43 to 2:50 contains
  any of "Where we live is a simulated universe / the thing that came down… / every traveler… /
  every light that went out…". The track realises the **continuation ending**, once:
  paper → maybe → both → whisper → still loading → long decay.

  The plan therefore gives F01–F03 no singing, captions or mouth shapes. Their ideas become a
  wordless visual echo inside the 2:43.5–2:50.0 break (see S20). Nothing is duplicated or extended.
- **D2. The chorus 1 refrain sounds like "unfinished", not "unanswered"** to the recogniser, in three
  overlapping windows (1:10–1:15). Chorus 2's "unfinished" (2:09) is clear. Design still follows the
  author's meaning (an unanswered frontier). Until someone listens and confirms, that word gets no
  typography and no caption.
- **D3. Voice roles** (B leads and A answers, B spoken, A whisper, both) are the author's intentions. A
  single separated vocal cannot confirm who sings. Mouth animation is assigned by the author roles.

## 2. Honest capability statement

**What my current pipeline does well:**
- painted or light environments in 2.5D, with depth, parallax, fog and light shafts;
- dimensional state changes of the world (sphere → plane → line, a projection front moving through a
  scene);
- particle systems that obey a scene's perspective;
- continuity across cuts;
- frame-accurate timing to the vocal;
- compositing.

It can also build a proper limited-animation face rig on the supplied drawings: chart mouths placed
with jaw and cheek follow-through, side-view triptychs, eyelids, breathing, and hair and tail lag.
That meets the standard of anime close-up singing and dialogue, provided every head angle has
matching mouth drawings.

**What it cannot do:**
- produce continuous character action from these PNGs: turning a head through new angles, a hand
  travelling with the wrist, elbow and shoulder following, a seated body shifting weight, entering
  and settling an embrace, walking or floating with weight, or a person's own drawn deformation
  through a dimensional change.

Moving, warping or crossfading the existing pictures is exactly what the handoff rejects, and I
will not present it as animation. Those shots need **new continuous drawings**.

- R2a for the body action, piloted on the four feasibility gates first. Switch to R2b for any shot
  where R2a fails acceptance twice.
- R3 for about 9 plates (see §5).

In seconds of footage this is about 55 s of character action (12 shots, attempts excluded), about
70 s of face-rig close-ups, and the rest is world and dimensional shots.

I can't quote R2a prices from here. If each action shot needs about 4 attempts, budget for roughly
220 s of generated video. Get a quote for that from the chosen service before approving.

1. **G1, seated interaction:** S01, 0:05.7–0:13.6.

3. **G3, room arrival with desk occlusion:** S15, 1:57.2–2:02.0.

For each gate I would deliver:
- a layout;
- an exposure (timing) sheet locked to the cues above;
- the key-pose list (existing anchors plus the exact new drawings needed);
- the R2a key-frame pairs and prompts.

After the drawings or clips come back, I would assemble a low-resolution pose test or animatic
(stationary camera, original audio) for review.

**Acceptance criteria for every action test:**
- contact with the support (hips, hands, feet);
- weight shift and its shadow;
- intermediate anatomy through turns;
- hair and tail lag and settle;
- identity unchanged (horns, clip, star, eye colour, hair colour);
- faces always intact;
- correct occlusion.
